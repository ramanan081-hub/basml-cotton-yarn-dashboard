SECTION 1 — GLOBAL RAW COTTON: TYPES & CURRENT PRICES
Cotton is the world's most widely used natural fiber. It is classified globally by staple length, grade (color, brightness,
purity), and character (fiber strength and uniformity). The four major global cotton categories are: Extra-Long Staple
(ELS), Long Staple (LS), Medium Staple, and Short Staple. Prices are quoted on ICE Futures (US cents/lb) and
benchmarked by the IMF Primary Commodity Price index.

SECTION 2 — INDIAN RAW COTTON: TYPES & PRICES
India is the world's largest cotton producer, accounting for ~23% of global output. Indian cotton is classified into four
staple-length categories. Each variety grows in specific states and is suited to different yarn counts and textile
applications. India produced ~320.5 lakh bales in 2025-26 (revised upward). The government sets Minimum Support
Prices (MSP) through the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs.

SECTION 3 — GLOBAL YARN MARKET: TYPES & PRICES
Yarn is produced from cotton and other fibers through spinning processes. Key types include ring-spun, open-end (OE),
compact, air-jet, and vortex yarns. International yarn prices are tracked by ICE, EmergingTextiles.com, and Textile
Exchange. Counts (Ne) indicate fineness — higher Ne = finer yarn.

SECTION 4 — INDIAN YARN MARKET: TYPES & DOMESTIC PRICES
India is the world's largest exporter of cotton yarn, with a market valued at USD 10.16 billion in 2024, projected to grow to
USD 22.5 billion by 2035 (CAGR 7.4%). Major spinning hubs: Coimbatore (TN), Ludhiana (Punjab), Ahmedabad
(Gujarat), Mumbai/Ichalkaranji (Maharashtra), Rajkot (Gujarat), Bhiwani (Haryana). India exports yarn to China,
Bangladesh, Vietnam, Pakistan, Indonesia, Turkey, and Europe.
