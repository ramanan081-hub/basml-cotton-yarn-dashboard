COTTON ANALYSIS REPORT:
GLOBAL, INDIA & TAMIL NADU (2024–2026)
Global Cotton Overview
The global cotton market is currently characterized by firm baseline support, driven by geopolitical
realities, supply chain frictions, and inflation in agricultural inputs. As of Q2 2026, the market has
transitioned away from the massive surpluses of previous years into a more tightly balanced
environment. Energy sector volatility, specifically disruptions around key maritime chokepoints, has
increased the cost of synthetic alternatives like polyester, ensuring that global demand for natural fiber
remains highly resilient.
Production, Demand, and Stock Trends
For the 2025/26 season, global production has been revised slightly upward to 26.5 Million Metric Tons
(MMT). Concurrently, global mill-use (demand) is sustained at 25.9 MMT. This near-parity prevents
significant stock accumulation, with ending stocks projected at 16.8 MMT. Crucially, a large portion of
these ending stocks resides in inaccessible state reserves, making the functional commercial free-float
much tighter than the headline numbers suggest.
Key Insights for Market Participants
Structural Price Support: High fertilizer and diesel costs establish an elevated break-even price
for farmers globally, creating a high "floor" for international benchmark prices.
Speculative Inflows: Managed money and speculative funds have aggressively covered their
historical short positions, transitioning to a net-long stance and injecting upward momentum into
ICE futures.
Regional Imbalances: While global numbers suggest a mild surplus, localized deficits in massive
spinning hubs (such as India) are forcing robust international trade flows and driving up landed
premiums.

India’s Role in Global Cotton
India is the critical fulcrum of the global cotton matrix. As the world's second-largest producer and
consumer, its domestic balance sheet sends immediate ripples through global benchmark pricing. For
the 2025/26 season, India exhibits a structural deficit: domestic consumption (25.5 Million Bales)
significantly outpaces domestic production (23.8 Million Bales). Consequently, India's role has shifted
aggressively toward being a net importer, absorbing 4.2 Million Bales from the global market.
Key Market Trends
Import Dependency: The massive 40% year-over-year surge in Indian imports is the defining
characteristic of the 2025/26 season.
Firm Local Prices: Indian spot prices (e.g., Shankar-6) remain exceptionally firm, closely trailing
international parity.
Reduced Export Footprint: India’s raw cotton exports have contracted sharply to 1.2 Million
Bales to reserve fiber for value-added manufacturing.
Tamil Nadu Importance (Textile Demand Center)
Tamil Nadu (TN) is the undisputed textile engine of India, housing approximately 45% of the
nation's spinning capacity. However, TN produces a negligible amount of cotton (approx. 0.5
Million Bales). The state's massive spinning infrastructure—centered around Coimbatore, Tirupur,
and Dindigul—requires over 11.5 Million Bales annually. This immense disparity makes TN the
primary demand driver within India, heavily reliant on inter-state arrivals from Gujarat and
Maharashtra, as well as international imports arriving via VOC Port (Tuticorin).

PAGE 14: WEATHER IMPACT & PRODUCTION RISK
Meteorological Conditions
Rainfall & Temperature Status
Global weather modeling is indicating a transition toward La Niña. While this historically supports the
Indian Southwest Monsoon, current pre-monsoon temperatures in Central India have been
exceptionally high, heavily depleting soil moisture reserves. Conversely, the US Southwest faces a
severe, persistent drought.
Risk Factors & Production Impact
United States: The lack of subsoil moisture in West Texas threatens massive abandonment rates.
If May rains fail, global supply models will lose substantial high-grade fiber volumes, triggering
immediate price spikes.
India: The 2026/27 sowing entirely depends on the timely onset of the monsoon in early June.
Given the current tight balance sheet, a delayed monsoon will ignite panic buying by domestic
mills.
Upcoming Risks
Because the current market operates in a functional deficit (excluding state reserves), there is zero
margin for error. A weather anomaly will immediately transform the current "cost-push" rally into a
severe "supply-shock" bull market, deeply impacting the profitability of the textile sector.

Future Prediction
Short-Term (1-3 Months): Volatile, upward-trending market. Geopolitical risk premiums and the
physical lean season will force prices higher globally.
Medium-Term (3-6 Months): Price action depends entirely on weather. Assuming normal weather,
prices will plateau as new Northern Hemisphere crops arrive in October. If weather fails, expect
explosive breakouts.
Strategic Procurement
Trader Strategy
Utilize a "Buy-on-Dips" approach. The massive shift in speculative positioning dictates the market has
strong underlying support. Accumulate long positions during technical pullbacks and monitor INR/USD
currency pairs carefully.
Mill Purchase Planning (Focus: Tamil Nadu Mills)
Mills in Coimbatore, Tirupur, and across TN must secure 60-90 days of forward physical cover
immediately. The availability of clean, contamination-free domestic cotton from Gujarat will evaporate
by late May. Execute forward import contracts now via VOC Port (Tuticorin) for July/August delivery to
bridge the massive 11.5 MB supply gap.
Risk Management & Opportunity Zones
Mills should execute currency hedges against import contracts. Opportunity zones exist for mills that
can pivot to dynamic blending (cotton-polyester); by managing the blend ratio dynamically, they can
protect spinning margins during severe raw cotton price spikes.
