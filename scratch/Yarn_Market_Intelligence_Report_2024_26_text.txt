1.1 Market Overview
The global textile yarn market is valued at USD 82.4 billion in 2025 and is estimated to reach USD 86.2 billion in 2026,
growing at a CAGR of 5.0% through 2034 to reach USD 127.4 billion. Asia-Pacific dominates with a 34.6% market share,
led by China and India. Synthetic yarns (polyester, nylon, acrylic) account for approximately 47% of total production,
while cotton yarn remains critical for export markets including Bangladesh, Vietnam, and Egypt. Sustainability-driven
demand, recycled fiber adoption, and automation in spinning are the three major structural forces reshaping competition
in 2025-26.

SECTION 4 | GLOBAL PROBLEMS,
RISKS & ISSUES
Headwinds Impacting Yarn Manufacturing
& Trade 2025-26
n 1. US TARIFF
ESCALATION
High US import tariffs on Chinese and South Asian textiles have caused brands
to place smaller, fragmented orders rather than bulk seasonal orders, disrupting
production planning and reducing mill utilisation in India and Bangladesh.
US-China trade tensions add 15-25% cost premium.
n 2. COTTON PRICE
VOLATILITY
Raw cotton prices surged over 20% in 2022 and remain unstable. The Indian
government's reimposition of import duty on long-staple cotton (Jan 2026) has
raised input costs for mills and restricted access to cheaper high-quality foreign
cotton, squeezing margins 2-4%.
n 3. SOUTHEAST ASIA
OVERCAPACITY
Capacity additions in Vietnam, Indonesia, and Bangladesh have created yarn
oversupply in commodity counts, compressing spreads globally. Chinese
greenfield spinning investments in Africa (Ethiopia, Egypt) are further reshaping
competitive cost curves.
n 4. SUSTAINABILITY
COMPLIANCE COSTS
EU Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations require 30% reduction in
water and chemical use. EUDR (deforestation regulation) and CBAM (carbon
border adjustment) add compliance costs of 5-8% for exporters targeting
European markets from 2026.
n 5. SUPPLY CHAIN
DISRUPTIONS
Red Sea shipping crisis has raised freight costs by 40-60% on Asia-Europe
routes. Vessel diversions via Cape of Good Hope add 10-14 days transit time,
affecting just-in-time delivery models and forcing higher inventory buffers.
n 6. CURRENCY &
FOREX RISKS
INR depreciated 3% since Aug 2025 – a double-edged effect: export
competitiveness improved but import costs for raw materials (cotton, chemicals,
machinery spares) rose. Turkish lira and Pakistani rupee instability further
distorts regional price benchmarks.
n 7. LABOUR & ENERGY
COST PRESSURES
Rising minimum wages across manufacturing hubs in India, Bangladesh, and
Vietnam are increasing production costs. Electricity tariffs for textile clusters in
Tamil Nadu rose 8-12% in 2025, pressuring small-medium spinning mills
operating on thin margins.
n 8. BANGLADESH LDC
GRADUATION
Bangladesh's graduation from LDC status in 2026 changes its tariff position,
potentially creating opportunities for India to capture EU/US market share
previously held by Bangladesh under zero-duty preferential access.

6.1 CCI Role & 2025-26 Procurement Overview
The Cotton Corporation of India (CCI) under the Ministry of Textiles acts as the central MSP (Minimum Support Price)
enforcement agency. CCI procures seed cotton (Kapas) when market prices fall below MSP levels and sells through
e-auctions to institutional buyers, MSME mills, and traders. CCI procurement in 2025-26 has crossed 13.4 million 480lb
bales – the second highest since 2019-20 – directly impacting mill input costs and spinner profitability.

6.2 CCI Impact on Tamil Nadu Spinners
n POSITIVE IMPACTS
• Stable cotton availability through e-auctions reduces sourcing risk for TN's 2,032 mills
• Price floor prevents cotton from crashing, protecting farmer income and crop area continuity
• CCI's 2025-26 procurement of 13.4M bales – 4% higher than last year – ensures adequate supply
• Spinners' profitability boosted by 50-100 basis points (CRISIL FY26 estimate)
• E-auction transparency enables better planning for MSME spinners in Coimbatore, Tirupur, Erode
n RISK FACTORS
• CCI's 6.8M unsold bales create sudden price-dump risk when liquidated in bulk
• MSP increase of 8.3% (medium staple) raises input costs for mills buying at MSP
• January 2026 import duty reinstatement on long-staple cotton reduces blending flexibility
• Over-reliance on CCI supply creates vulnerability if procurement policy changes
• Telangana & Maharashtra dominate CCI procurement; transport costs add 3-5% for TN mills

SECTION 13 | ANALYTICAL
METHODS & MODELS USED
Statistical Frameworks | Forecasting
Techniques | Data Sources
n TIME SERIES
ANALYSIS
Year-on-Year (YoY) comparison using 12-month rolling averages. Seasonal
decomposition applied to identify Aug-Nov peak and Feb-Mar off-peak patterns.
CAGR calculated across 5-year base (2019-20 to 2025-26) for benchmark
verification.
n SUPPLY-DEMAND
MODELLING
Input-Output matrix linking cotton fiber procurement → yarn production → fabric →
garment export. Inventory buffer analysis using CCI stock data, mill utilisation rates
(90% reported for FY26) and shipping lead times. Gap analysis identifies
surplus/deficit zones.
n REGRESSION &
FORECASTING
Linear regression on 3-year monthly production data to project Jun-Nov 2026.
ARIMA (1,1,1) seasonal model applied to export volume data. Monte Carlo
simulation used for scenario analysis (Bull/Base/Bear probability distributions).
n PRICE PATTERN
ANALYSIS
Cotton ICE futures correlation with Indian domestic MCX prices. 90-day moving
average applied to Rs/kg domestic yarn prices. INR/USD exchange rate sensitivity
analysis showing 1% INR depreciation → ~1.2% export competitiveness
improvement.
n CLUSTER ANALYSIS Geographic cluster scoring for Tamil Nadu's Textile Valley
(Coimbatore-Tirupur-Erode-Karur belt). Porter's Diamond model applied to assess
competitive advantage factors: factor conditions, demand, related industries, and
firm strategy.
n CCI IMPACT
DIFFERENTIAL
Diff-in-Diff (Difference-in-Differences) approach comparing mill profitability metrics
between CCI-procured cotton users vs open-market sourcing mills. CRISIL data
calibrated against actual Q1-Q3 FY26 results.
n RISK ASSESSMENT PESTEL framework applied to global risk factors. Heat mapping of 8 identified risks
across probability (Low/Medium/High) x Impact (1-5 scale). Red Sea freight impact
modelled as 40-60% cost increase on Asia-Europe routes.
