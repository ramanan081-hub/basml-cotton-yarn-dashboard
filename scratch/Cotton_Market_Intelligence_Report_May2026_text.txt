l 01 n GLOBAL COTTON MARKET OVERVIEW & PRODUCTION FORECAST
Global cotton markets in May 2026 are exhibiting robust bullish momentum, with ICE Cotton No.2 futures touching 86.8
cents/lb, the highest level since April 2024, representing a remarkable +24.7% year-on-year gain. The Cotlook A Index
stands at 97.65 cents/lb, signalling tight premium market conditions. Multiple demand-supply dynamics are converging:
geopolitical tensions have elevated crude oil prices, which in turn increases polyester production costs and makes cotton a
more attractive fibre substitute. Simultaneously, USDA's latest WASDE (May 2026) indicates a tighter 2026/27 supply outlook
with lower forecast production of 13.3 million bales for the United States.

l 03 n INDIA COTTON SEASON 2025/26 — DEEP DIVE
India's cotton sector in 2025/26 presents a net positive but nuanced picture. The Cotton Association of India (CAI)
progressively revised its production estimate upward through the season — from an initial 305 lakh bales in November 2025
to 320.5 lakh bales (March 2026 revision, latest available). This compares favourably to 312.4 lakh bales in 2024/25. Strong
crop performance in Maharashtra (+3 LB), Andhra Pradesh, and continued robust output from Gujarat drove the upward
revisions. The Government of India raised the Minimum Support Price (MSP) for cotton for 2026/27 — Medium Staple to
n8,267/quintal (from n7,710) and Long Staple to n8,667/quintal (from n8,110), offering meaningful price support to
farmers.

El Niño Alert for 2026: A strong El Niño is expected to emerge in 2026, bringing hotter and drier weather across Asia —
particularly concerning for India, where 65% of cotton area is rainfed. Historical precedent (2014/15 and 2018/19 El Niño
years) saw Indian cotton production decline 8–12%. Early preparedness includes promoting drought-tolerant varieties,
micro-irrigation expansion under PM Krishi Sinchai Yojana, and crop insurance coverage under PMFBY. The Cotton Mission
announced in Budget 2025/26 allocates n500 crore over 5 years for climate resilience research and farmer capacity building
— a positive step but requiring accelerated implementation ahead of the anticipated El Niño impact.

Strategic Recommendations:
• For Mills / Spinners (India): Capitalize on comfortable domestic availability and slightly softer Indian prices to build
strategic raw material inventory before El Niño impact materializes in 2026/27.
• For Exporters: Focus on value-added yarn and fabric exports rather than raw cotton. Bangladesh and Vietnam remain
strong buyers. Explore premium organic cotton opportunities.
• For Farmers: Consider switching to ELS/Long Staple varieties supported by Cotton Mission. Adopt drip irrigation (50%
subsidy available) to hedge against El Niño drought risk.
• For Traders/Investors: Bullish on ICE cotton near-term. Watch Texas crop progress closely (planting at 29% of normal
as of May 10, 2026). El Niño confirmation would be a strong buy signal.
• For Policy: Fast-track Cotton Mission implementation; extend import duty waiver monitoring; consider building buffer
stock at CCI to prevent excessive stock overhang depressing farmer prices.
