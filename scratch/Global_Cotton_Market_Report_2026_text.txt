1. Latest International Cotton Market News (2025–2026)
Brazil Secures Lead: In a historic shift for the 2025/26 season, Brazil has consolidated its
position as the world’s top cotton exporter, leveraging massive infrastructure investments in the
Mato Grosso region.
Inventory Surplus: Global ending stocks have reached a three-year high due to a slight
contraction in global yarn demand, leading to downward pressure on ICE futures.
Technological Leap: China has implemented wide-scale AI-driven irrigation in Xinjiang, resulting
in record-breaking yields per hectare despite stable planting areas.
Sustainability Mandates: New EU textile regulations (ESPR) have increased the price premium
for "Better Cotton" and Organic certified lint by 12% over conventional grades.
2. Nation-to-Nation Price Differences
Cotton prices currently exhibit significant regional variance due to the following factors:
Logistics Premium: Australian cotton maintains a premium due to its proximity to Southeast
Asian spinning hubs (Vietnam/Indonesia).
Government Support: China’s prices remain artificially high compared to the global A-Index due
to the Minimum Support Price (MSP) and state reserve replenishment.
Currency Devaluation: Lower prices in Pakistan and Brazil are driven by weaker local
currencies against the USD, making their exports highly competitive.

4. Cotton Harvest Analysis
Highest Producing Countries
China (1st): Estimated 6.1 Million Metric Tons (MMT). High yield efficiency.
India (2nd): Estimated 5.8 MMT. Large acreage but yield remains weather-dependent.
Brazil (3rd): Estimated 3.6 MMT. Driven by "Safrinha" (second-crop) expansion.
Lowest Producing Countries (Key Exporters)
Greece: Significant decline due to high energy costs and land diversion to food crops.
Uzbekistan: Reduced export availability as the nation prioritizes value-added domestic textile
manufacturing.

6. Impact of Global Issues
Russia–Ukraine War: Continued impact on fertilizer costs; however, alternative supply chains
from Morocco and Canada have stabilized prices in 2026.
Gaza & Red Sea Crisis: Freight rates for India-to-Europe routes remain 25% higher than 2023
levels due to insurance surcharges and longer transit times around the Cape of Good Hope.
US–China Trade: Ongoing high-tech sanctions have led China to diversify its cotton imports
further toward Brazil and African origins.
Climate Change: Erratic monsoon patterns in India and drought in West Texas caused a 5%
volatility spike in cotton futures during Q1 2026.
7. Impact on Global Exports and Imports
Exports: The US market share is under pressure. Brazil now commands the Southeast Asian
market. India’s exports are restricted by high domestic consumption.
Imports: Bangladesh and Vietnam remain the "engines of demand," accounting for nearly 35%
of global imports to fuel their garment export sectors.
8. Summary, Key Risks, and Future Outlook
Summary: The global market is currently "Neutral-to-Bearish." Supply is plentiful, but demand is
cautious due to global inflation affecting consumer garment spending.
Key Risks:
Sudden escalation in Middle East tensions affecting oil (synthetic fiber) prices.
Potential "La Niña" weather event impacting the 2026 US harvest.
Future Outlook: We anticipate prices to consolidate at 75–80 cents/lb through Q3 2026. Industry
buyers are advised to adopt a "covered" position for immediate needs while waiting for new-crop
clarity before long-term booking.
